# Category 5 Contrast Table: Token-Efficient Reasoning & Budget-Aware Inference

| Paper | Our Motivation (cost-aware stopping) | Our Approach (monitor agent as reward model) | Our Solution (learned soft stopping curve) | Key Difference |
|-------|--------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|----------------|
| **Compute-Optimal Inference (Snell)** | Per-prompt adaptive allocation is necessary — foundational motivation for dynamic stopping. | Uses task-specific PRM for quality scoring — no separate monitor, no learned stopping. | Offline-optimized allocation policy — not a learned, real-time stopping curve. | Offline optimization vs. online learned monitor; we use a separate model, not a task-specific PRM. |
| **Inference Scaling Laws (Wu)** | Diminishing returns universally observed — accuracy plateaus with more compute. | No method proposed — empirical analysis only. | N/A — establishes that diminishing returns exist but doesn't solve detection. | Proves the problem; our monitor provides the detection mechanism. |
| **TALE** | Token budgets matter and per-problem estimation is valuable — direct motivation. | Prompt-based budget enforcement — no learned model, no reward signals. | Static pre-estimated budget — not a dynamic, observation-based stopping curve. | Prompt-based static budget vs. learned dynamic monitor; our approach is more robust and adaptive. |
| **SelfBudgeter** | Learns to estimate and adhere to token budgets — closely aligned with our goal. | Trains the policy model itself — no separate monitor. Self-estimated budgets before generation. | Static pre-estimated budget + length penalty — not real-time adaptive stopping. | Policy-internal efficiency vs. external monitor; our monitor works with any base model. |
| **BudgetThinker** | Continuous budget signaling during generation — complementary to our monitor's role. | Trains budget awareness into the policy via control tokens — no separate monitor. | Fixed budget with control token adherence — not a learned dynamic stopping curve. | Budget awareness embedded in the policy; we use a separate monitor for more flexibility. |
| **DiffAdapt** | U-shaped entropy pattern — a signal our monitor could use to detect overthinking. | Lightweight probe classifier for difficulty — no monitor, no stopping decisions. | Three discrete difficulty tiers with fixed strategies — not a continuous stopping curve. | Difficulty classification vs. real-time stopping; our monitor uses richer signals. |
| **L1 (LCPO)** | Length-constrained RL training — shows models can learn budget awareness. | Trains budget adherence into the policy itself — no separate monitor. Prompt-specified budget | User-specified budget constraint — not a learned, context-aware stopping decision. | User vs. monitor decides the budget; our approach is autonomous and context-aware. |
| **Reason Efficiently (Arora)** | Simplest RL-for-efficiency approach — directly informs our reward design. | Trains the policy model with a linear length penalty — no separate monitor. | Linear length penalty (α * length) — a static trade-off, not a learned dynamic curve. | Static penalty vs. learned monitor; our α is effectively dynamic and context-dependent. |

**Summary:** All papers in this category optimize for token efficiency but do so by either (a) embedding budget awareness into the policy model itself through RL with length penalties, or (b) using prompt-based or heuristic budget control. None provides a **separate, learned monitor** that makes real-time, context-dependent stopping decisions. Our proposal replaces the static α or pre-estimated budget with a dynamic, observation-based stopping signal from a trained monitor — enabling per-instance adaptation rather than one-size-fits-all efficiency.
