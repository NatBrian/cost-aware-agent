# Category 6 Contrast Table: Efficient Tool Use & Long-Horizon/Deep Research Tasks

| Paper | Our Motivation (cost-aware stopping) | Our Approach (monitor agent as reward model) | Our Solution (learned soft stopping curve) | Key Difference |
|-------|--------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|----------------|
| **Plan-and-Act** | Planner-executor separation mirrors our monitor-executor split. Dynamic replanning is analogous to "adjust approach." | Planner is an LLM generating structured plans — no separate cost-aware monitor, no reward model. | Replanning triggers are based on task failure, not cost-benefit analysis — no learned stopping curve. | Plan revision vs. cost-aware stopping; our monitor adds the cost dimension to the replanning decision. |
| **Evolution of Tool Use** | Explicitly identifies the lack of cost-aware tool orchestration as a gap — directly motivating our work. | Survey paper — no method proposed. | N/A — identifies the gap that our proposal fills. | Diagnostic survey; our work provides the cost-aware orchestration it calls for. |
| **To Call or Not to Call** | Cost-benefit analysis for individual tool-call decisions — directly analogous to our stopping analysis. | Learned cost model for tool calls — no separate monitor, no reward signals for executor training. | Per-call cost-benefit optimization — not a learned, trajectory-level stopping curve. | Per-call decisions vs. overall trajectory stopping; we aggregate across all cost dimensions. |
| **DeepResearcher** | Deep research tasks are the ideal testbed — agent learns to stop searching implicitly via outcome RL. | Outcome-based GRPO training — no separate monitor, no explicit stopping reward. | Implicit stopping learned through task completion — not an explicit, trained stopping mechanism. | Implicit vs. explicit stopping; our monitor makes stopping a first-class training objective. |
| **ReTool** | RL teaches emergent when-to-use-tools behavior — parallels our goal of teaching when-to-stop. | Outcome-based GRPO on code execution tasks — no separate monitor, no cost-awareness. | Emergent tool-use strategy — not a learned stopping curve; no explicit cost consideration. | Tool use vs. stopping; our monitor is explicitly trained for cost-aware stopping. |
| **ToolRL** | Systematic reward design approach — directly informs our reward function design for cost-awareness. | Multi-component reward (accuracy + format + execution) — no cost component, no separate monitor. | Static multi-component reward — not a learned, dynamic stopping signal. | Task quality reward design vs. cost-quality trade-off reward; we add a cost dimension. |
| **RRO** | Rising reward trajectory concept — diminishing deltas signal when to stop. | Process supervision with DPO — no separate monitor, no cost-awareness. | Rising reward signal identifies quality segments — not a cost-benefit stopping curve. | Quality-focused reward trends; we add cost to the analysis. |
| **CARL** | Critical action identification — "stop vs. continue" is a critical action under CARL's framework. | Model confidence as criticality proxy — no separate monitor, no cost-awareness. | Selective optimization on critical steps — not a learned stopping curve. | General criticality vs. stopping-specific criticality; our monitor is a specialized criticality estimator. |

**Summary:** This category represents the primary **application domain** for our monitor agent. Long-horizon tasks with expensive tool calls are where cost-aware stopping has the highest impact. Current approaches either learn tool-use efficiency implicitly through outcome RL (DeepResearcher, ReTool), optimize tool-call decisions individually (To Call or Not to Call), or use reward trends to identify quality segments (RRO). None uses a separate monitor to make explicit, cost-aware stopping decisions. Our proposal fills this gap — making stopping a first-class training objective rather than an emergent side effect of outcome optimization.
