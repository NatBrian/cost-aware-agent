# Novelty Check — Agent 1: Skeptical ICLR Area Chair

> Reviewer stance: I have handled hundreds of "efficient agent / efficient reasoning" submissions since 2025. Most get rejected for one of three reasons: (a) the claimed gap was already filled 6–12 months earlier, (b) the "novel framework" is a re-labeled iterated actor-critic, (c) training-free baselines match the trained pipeline. I evaluated CASSI against all three. Date of review: 2026-07-16. Sources: `research/paper_plan.md` (read in full, all 1576 lines) + my own fresh searches (log below). I did not read any prior internal novelty conclusions.

---

## My understanding of the proposal

CASSI targets the "economic judgment" gap in LLM agents: agents trained to be helpful have no reward-consistent notion of "good enough, stop now," so they overthink, loop, or (under naive penalties) stop prematurely. The proposed fix is a two-model training pipeline: (1) run the executor (7B–72B) to completion on training tasks; (2) compute a post-hoc "oracle" stopping point per trajectory, `t* = argmax_t [quality_t − λ·cumulative_cost_{1..t}]`, an O(T) computation requiring no additional policy executions (contrasted with AgentPRM-style Monte Carlo rollouts at O(K×T²)); (3) train a small stopping model (0.5B–3B) on these labels via SFT + GRPO to emit STOP/CONTINUE/ADJUST plus a continuous cost-aware value Δ(s_t) = Q_continue − Q_stop; (4) use Δ(s_t) as a step-level process reward to train the executor with GRPO, so the executor internalizes cost-awareness rather than being externally truncated. The loop ①collect→②oracle→③train stopper→④stopper-as-PRM→⑤executor RL→back to ① is branded "the self-reinforcing cycle" and claimed as the primary contribution, with an ablation (stopper-as-controller-only) designed to prove the ④→⑤ bridge is necessary.

The five claimed contributions are: (C1) the first closed loop of this kind; (C2) theoretical + empirical necessity of a *separate* stopping model due to representation conflict between execution and economic self-evaluation; (C3) O(T) oracle stopping labels replacing O(K×T²) MC rollouts, with three formal properties (monotonicity, λ-sensitivity, convergence under policy improvement); (C4) per-instance, mid-trajectory cost adaptation beating static length penalties (tested via difficulty–stopping-point correlation, H5: r > 0.5); (C5) a tiny stopper supervising large executors at <3% overhead with 20–40% cost savings. Evaluation is planned on GAIA, WebWalkerQA, HotpotQA, MuSiQue, SWE-bench Verified, MATH-500 (control), BFCL, against ~14 baselines including CaRT+cost+GRPO, AgentPRM-cost, adaptive-α penalties, and single-model GRPO+cost. The plan is unusually self-aware (P0 must-pass baselines, acknowledged oracle-label noise, MC-parallelization caveat), which I credit — but self-awareness does not immunize against the 2025–2026 literature, which has moved fast.

## Core claims extracted (numbered)

1. **C1 — The cycle:** The closed loop (oracle labels → stopping model → cost-aware process rewards → executor GRPO → better trajectories → repeat) "does not exist in any prior work."
2. **C2 — Separate-model necessity:** A dedicated stopping model is structurally necessary (representation conflict); single-model cost penalties "fundamentally fail"; ablation predicts strict Pareto ordering (separate > multi-task single > penalty single).
3. **C3 — O(T) oracle labels:** Optimal stopping labels computable analytically from completed trajectories (zero extra executions), vs. O(K×T²) MC rollouts for existing agent PRMs; plus three formal properties.
4. **C4 — Per-instance, mid-trajectory adaptation:** Learned stopping adapts cost pressure to task difficulty and observed progress, beating static/instance-blind penalties.
5. **C5 — Small-supervises-large:** A 0.5B–3B stopper supervising 7B–72B executors at <3% inference overhead is a contribution-grade capability.
6. **C6 (implicit, load-bearing):** Cost-aware stopping value (quality − λ·cost margin) as a *process reward for training the executor* — i.e., cost-awareness gets internalized into the policy, not enforced at runtime.

## Per-claim novelty verdicts

**C1 — the self-reinforcing cycle: LOW novelty as claimed; MEDIUM for the specific instantiation.**
Closest: AgentPRM-Choudhury (explicitly *iterative* PRM↔policy training), iStar (*"self-reinforcing training loop"*, verbatim), AgentPRM-Fudan (PRM → agent RL). The plan itself concedes in §10.1 that the loop is "standard iterative actor-critic, following the PRM training pattern from AgentPRM" — which directly contradicts contribution 1's "no prior work closes this loop." What is genuinely unclaimed: the critic being a *cost-aware stopping-value* model. Delta = re-targeting a known loop at a new signal. That is a contribution, but not "first cycle."

**C2 — separate stopping model necessity: LOW for the architecture, MEDIUM for the ablation result (if it holds).**
Closest: TAB (separate GRPO-trained cost controller, mid-trajectory), Ares (separate effort router for agents), SupervisorAgent (separate runtime monitor), AgentRM (separate RM guiding agents), plus PPG establishing policy/value interference in 2020, plus ReMA (the plan's own citation) for meta/executor decoupling. "Separate small controller beside a big executor" is now a standard pattern. The *strict Pareto ordering* single-vs-multi-task-vs-separate ablation would be a nice empirical finding, but "theoretical justification" is overclaimed — there is no theory in the plan, only an intuition that PPG articulated six years ago.

**C3 — O(T) oracle labels vs O(K×T²) MC: LOW-MEDIUM.**
The "MC rollouts are the only existing way, hence PRM training is intractable" premise is a strawman as of late 2025: InversePRM (in the very paper the plan cites!), TD+GAE (AgentPRM-Fudan, 8× efficiency, WWW 2026), implicit PRMs (iStar/OPRL, PRIME), SPA-RL, GiGPO, and Progress Advantage all obtain step-level signals without O(K×T²) rollouts. The narrower trick — hindsight optimal-*stopping* labels from completed traces — exists in TERMINATOR (first-answer-position labels, CoT) and in spirit in Ares (hindsight minimum-effort labels, agents) and web-agent stop-action relabeling (2602.12544). The surviving delta: an explicit `quality − λ·cost` argmax stopping oracle with multi-dimensional (token/tool/dollar) cost on tool-use agents. Real but thin, and the "formal properties" are trivial comparative statics (Property 1's uniqueness claim is actually not even correct without a diminishing-returns assumption; ties and multi-modal f(t) are possible). Also note: on SWE-bench, computing `quality_t` at every step means running the test suite at every step — the oracle is *not* "zero additional execution" there; it trades policy rollouts for T evaluator executions per trajectory. A careful reviewer will catch this.

**C4 — per-instance, mid-trajectory adaptation beats static penalties: MEDIUM-LOW.**
Per-instance adaptive budgets are established in reasoning (ALP, IBPO, LASER-D, DAST, AdaCtrl, e1); mid-trajectory adaptive control exists for multi-turn/agents (TAB per-turn budgets, Ares per-step effort, VOI per-step value-of-information, SupervisorAgent runtime interventions). The plan's Adaptive-α P0 baseline shows the authors sense this. The surviving delta: *learned termination* (not budget allocation) adapting mid-trajectory in tool agents, with difficulty-correlation analysis (H5). H5 itself (harder task → later stop) has been demonstrated qualitatively by DAST/AdaCtrl-style work in reasoning, so the novelty is the agent setting.

**C5 — small stopper supervises large executor, <3% overhead: LOW.**
Small-guides-large is routine (Fudan AgentPRM 3B PRM, AgentRM 8B, SupervisorAgent, TAB, Ares, speculative/cascade literature). This is a configuration, not a contribution. Worse, two 2026 papers give concrete evidence that monitor overhead accounting can flip conclusions (LearnStop: same policy saves 32% or costs +121% depending on serving infra; Semantic Early-Stopping: judge-based stopping is dominated by judge cost). The plan's overhead estimate counts only monitor *tokens*, not the cost of constructing its inputs (per-step answer extraction, quality/confidence/stability indicators — on SWE-bench, per-step test runs).

**C6 — stopping-value Δ as process reward to train the executor (cost internalization): HIGH-ish novelty — the one genuinely unoccupied cell.**
I found no work that trains an executor's policy with process rewards emitted by a *trained cost-aware stopping/value model*, such that cost-aware termination behavior is internalized rather than externally enforced. Closest misses: iStar (implicit PRM trains policy, no cost), TAB (cost controller, frozen solver), CaRT (trained stopper, no executor RL), OTC-style tool-cost penalties (single-model, static). The cycle-necessity ablation (controller-only vs process-reward bridge) is the most interesting scientific question in the plan. This is where the paper should plant its flag — currently it is buried as step ④→⑤ of an overclaimed "cycle."

## Overall assessment

- **Score: 4/10** (novelty as currently framed; would be ~5.5/10 reframed around C6 with honest baselines).
- **Recommendation: PROCEED WITH CAUTION** — substantial reframing and de-scoping required before any experiment is run.
- **Key differentiator (the thing to keep):** executor RL with process rewards from a trained cost-aware stopping-value model — "internalized economics" vs. runtime enforcement — plus the controller-only vs. reward-bridge ablation, and cost-λ stopping oracle labels for tool agents with tool/dollar (not just token) costs.
- **Biggest risk (what a reviewer will cite):** *"The 'self-reinforcing cycle' is iterated actor-critic (AgentPRM, iStar — the latter uses the exact phrase); cheap PRM labels without MC rollouts are well-established (TD+GAE AgentPRM WWW'26, implicit PRMs, InversePRM); and training-free monitors already achieve the promised savings on the same benchmarks (SupervisorAgent ICLR'26: −29.7% tokens on GAIA at parity; Dynasor: −50%; semantic stopping: −38% on HotpotQA). What remains is a cost term in a known pipeline."* If CASSI's numbers land at 20–40% savings, they are *indistinguishable from free methods* unless the paper proves an accuracy or Pareto advantage over them specifically.

## Weaknesses & likely rejection reasons (ranked, brutal)

1. **Contribution 1 is falsifiable as written.** "No prior work closes this loop" will be refuted in the first review round with iStar (verbatim "self-reinforcing training loop") and AgentPRM's iterative stages, and §10.1's own admission ("standard iterative actor-critic") reads as internal contradiction. An AC checks whether claims survive the paper's own text; this one doesn't.
2. **The O(K×T²) strawman.** The plan positions MC rollouts as the only existing labeling route, while the cited AgentPRM paper itself contains InversePRM (no rollouts), and WWW'26 AgentPRM does TD+GAE explicitly to avoid MC cost. Framing efficiency gains against the weakest known alternative is the classic overclaim pattern reviewers punish. The complexity accounting is also internally unsound on SWE-bench: `quality_t` per step = per-step test-suite execution, so "zero additional executions" is false in the flagship domain.
3. **Missing training-free baselines that already deliver the headline result.** SupervisorAgent (same venue, same benchmark, −29.7% at parity), Certaindex/Dynasor (NeurIPS'25, −50%), Semantic Early-Stopping (−38% on HotpotQA), TERMINATOR (−14–55%). None are in the baseline table. If a zero-training method matches a 3-stage RL pipeline (trajectory collection + stopper SFT/RL + executor GRPO), the pipeline is unjustified. The paper must beat these, not just ReAct/L1/CaRT.
4. **Overhead and oracle-cost accounting (soundness).** The monitor's *input construction* (per-step answer extraction, confidence, coverage, "progress rate," answer stability) is unpriced; LearnStop shows serving infrastructure alone can turn a 32% saving into a 121% cost increase. The "<3%" claim will not survive a careful efficiency reviewer without an end-to-end accounting that includes summarization, probing, and evaluator calls.
5. **Feasibility / scope collapse.** 7 benchmarks × ~14 baselines (several of which — BudgetThinker, ReMA-cost, AgentPRM-cost — require *reimplementing and retraining other people's methods*) × 9 ablations × 3 seeds, with agent GRPO on SWE-bench Verified, in an 8-week plan. This is 10–20× over-scoped. Reviewers never see the plan, but the paper that survives this collapse will have thin, cherry-looking results. SWE-bench GRPO alone (G=8, 20 steps, repo-scale envs) is a multi-week engineering project (cf. SWE-RL-scale efforts).
6. **"Theoretical justification" that isn't.** The three "formal properties" are one-line comparative statics; Property 1's uniqueness claim is incorrect without a diminishing-returns assumption (quality plateaus/ties break it); Property 3 ("oracle converges under policy improvement") is an intuition with no statement of conditions — and the known failure mode (oracle labels are only optimal *for the trajectory taken*, i.e., off-policy bias toward the current policy's habits) is acknowledged but not analyzed. Meanwhile Cognitive Friction (2603.30031) already gives an HJB optimal-stopping formalization for tool agents; a theory-flavored reviewer will unfavorably compare.
7. **C2's rhetoric outruns its evidence.** "Fundamentally fail," "structurally necessary," "no guarantee representations are compatible" — this is PPG's 2020 observation re-asserted, and the actual deliverable is one ablation. If the ablation shows parity (the plan's own Risk table rates this "Medium probability"), contributions 1 and 2 both collapse simultaneously — a single point of failure for half the paper.
8. **Stale related work for a mid-2026 submission.** The plan's most recent competitors are late-2025/early-2026; it misses TAB, Ares' hindsight-label pipeline (mischaracterized as "trial-and-error"), VOI budget control, Calibrate-Then-Act, LearnStop, BAGEN, SupervisorAgent's ICLR acceptance. At ICLR 2027 review time (Sept–Nov 2026), reviewers will hold it against the paper.

## Concrete improvement suggestions

1. **Re-headline around C6.** Title-level claim should be: *cost-aware stopping distilled into the executor via process rewards from a small trained stopping-value model* — "agents that internalize when to stop," not "the first self-reinforcing cycle." Demote the cycle to the training procedure section and cite iStar/AgentPRM as the loop template you specialize.
2. **Replace the O(K×T²) strawman with a 3-way label-efficiency comparison:** oracle stopping labels (yours) vs. MC-rollout labels vs. TD+GAE (Fudan AgentPRM) and/or implicit-PRM (iStar-style) labels, at matched compute, all with a cost term. If your oracle wins on stopping accuracy per GPU-hour, that's a defensible efficiency claim. Also fix the SWE-bench accounting: report oracle labeling cost *including* per-step quality evaluation, and consider sparse quality probes (every k steps) as a mitigation.
3. **Add the training-free monitor baselines as P0:** SupervisorAgent (2510.26585), a Dynasor/Certaindex-style answer-stability probe, and semantic-drift stopping (2606.27009). The paper's central table must show CASSI Pareto-dominating *these*, not just ReAct/L1/CaRT. If it can't, the honest paper is about when training helps (cf. 2606.30852's regime analysis) — which is also publishable, but different.
4. **Cut scope to 3 benchmarks (GAIA or WebWalkerQA, MuSiQue, SWE-bench-lite subset) + MATH-500 control; cut baselines to ~7** (ReAct, self-eval prompt, adaptive-α penalty, CaRT+cost+GRPO, single-model GRPO+cost, AgentPRM-cost-or-TD, SupervisorAgent). Keep the two decisive ablations: single-vs-two-model, and controller-only-vs-reward-bridge. Everything else is appendix material.
5. **Pre-register the two kill-switch experiments and run them FIRST at small scale:** (a) does Δ-as-process-reward beat Δ-as-controller-only on one benchmark with a 1.5B stopper + 7B executor? (b) does the two-model split beat single-model multi-task GRPO at matched parameter count (stopper params counted!)? If either fails, pivot before investing in SWE-bench infrastructure.
6. **Fix the honesty of C2:** cite PPG for the interference phenomenon, drop "theoretical," and frame as: "we test whether execution/valuation interference — known in classic RL — manifests in LLM agents' stopping decisions." Add the matched-parameter control (7B single multi-task vs. 7B executor + 1.5B stopper) or reviewers will attribute the win to extra capacity.
7. **End-to-end cost accounting protocol:** report total dollars/GPU-seconds including monitor inference, monitor input construction, oracle labeling, and all training stages, amortized per solved task; compare against the training-free baselines' zero amortization. Adopt LearnStop's "matched lost-correct risk" style reporting for stopping-policy fairness.
8. **Update related work for mid-2026** (TAB, Ares-as-hindsight-labels, VOI control, Calibrate-Then-Act, LearnStop, orchestration survey 2605.02801 — the last one is actually *favorable*: cite its "nobody trains the stop decision" finding as independent confirmation of the gap, and move fast, because that sentence is an open invitation to every reader).

## Is this strong enough for ICLR?

**As written: below the accept threshold — I would expect scores around 4/5/5/6 and rejection at ICLR 2027.** The area is one of the most crowded of 2025–26 (I verified ~30 directly adjacent papers in one afternoon, five from the last four months alone), and three of the five claimed contributions (cycle, O(T)-vs-O(K×T²), small-supervises-large) are each individually deflatable with a single citation the plan does not contain (iStar; Fudan AgentPRM/InversePRM; SupervisorAgent). Reviewers in this subfield are demonstrably citation-armed and fatigued by "efficient agent" framings; an AC will not fight for a paper whose novelty statement contradicts its own §10.1.

**The path to acceptance exists but is narrow.** The unoccupied cell is real: nobody trains an executor with process rewards from a trained cost-aware stopping-value model, and nobody has shown whether stopping economics can be *internalized* into a policy rather than enforced at runtime (the 2605.02801 survey independently confirms the stop decision is never an RL target). If the reframed paper (a) proves the reward-bridge is necessary (controller-only fails), (b) beats training-free monitors — SupervisorAgent, stability probes — on the cost-accuracy Pareto on ≥2 agent benchmarks including one long-horizon domain, and (c) presents honest end-to-end cost accounting including all monitor/labeling overheads, then it clears the bar as a solid empirical contribution (7/10 territory). Requirement (b) is the make-or-break unknown, and nothing in the plan guarantees it: training buys per-instance calibration, but free methods already harvest most of the slack on high-slack benchmarks. Run kill-switch experiments (suggestion 5) within the first two weeks; abandon or pivot to the "when does learned stopping help agents" study framing if they fail. Do not spend eight weeks building the full pipeline on the current claim structure.
