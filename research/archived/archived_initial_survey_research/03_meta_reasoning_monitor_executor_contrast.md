# Category 3 Contrast Table: Meta-Reasoning, Monitor-Executor Architectures & Metacognition

| Paper | Our Motivation (cost-aware stopping) | Our Approach (monitor agent as reward model) | Our Solution (learned soft stopping curve) | Key Difference |
|-------|--------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|----------------|
| **MGV** | Provides theoretical/cognitive grounding for why a monitor is necessary — the monitoring function is missing from Generate-Verify architectures. | Formal framework only — no implementation, no training, no reward model. | Resource-rational stopping grounded in optimal stopping theory — provides theoretical basis for our soft curve. | Position paper vs. our empirical proposal; we build on MGV's theoretical foundation. |
| **ReMA** | Closest architectural match — separate high-level meta-agent guides a low-level reasoning agent via RL. | The meta-agent provides strategic plans, not cost-aware stopping decisions. Trained via multi-agent RL, not as a reward model. | No stopping decisions — the meta-agent guides strategy, not when to stop. | Strategy guidance vs. cost-aware stopping; our monitor is a reward model, ReMA's meta-agent is a strategy provider. |
| **Structured Meta-Cognition** | Uses meta-cognition for strategy selection — adapts reasoning approach based on task characteristics. | Structured meta-cognitive state with efficiency-penalized RL — similar training approach. | Selects reasoning strategies, not stopping decisions. | Strategy selection vs. stopping decisions; we apply meta-cognition to the cost-benefit trade-off of continuing. |
| **Re-ReST** | Separate reflector model provides feedback on agent trajectories — similar to our monitor-executor structure. | SFT-based iterative training — no RL, no reward model. Reflector provides general feedback, not cost-aware signals. | No stopping decisions — reflector provides general improvement suggestions. | General feedback vs. cost-aware stopping signals; we use RL instead of SFT. |
| **Agent-R** | Uses MCTS to find "transition points" for reflection — analogous to our monitor's stopping evaluation points. | SFT on MCTS-generated reflection trajectories — no RL, no separate monitor model. | Transition points are heuristic, not learned — no cost-benefit trade-off. | Heuristic reflection points vs. learned stopping decisions; we use a separate monitor model. |
| **SELF** | Language feedback as training signal — our monitor could provide rich, interpretable feedback beyond scalar scores. | Self-generated critiques — no separate monitor, no RL, no cost-awareness. | No stopping decisions — iterative refinement of outputs. | Self-critique vs. external monitor; we focus on stopping, not refinement. |
| **PACER** | Peer-review mechanism — a separate evaluation step beyond self-evaluation that improves efficiency. | Training-free inference method — no learned model, no reward signals. | Consensus-based revision, not learned stopping — no cost-benefit curve. | Inference-time coordination vs. trained monitor; our approach learns optimal stopping from data. |
| **Budget Guidance** | Separate predictor estimates remaining computation and guides behavior — similar to our monitor's role. | Lightweight supervised predictor — no RL, no reward model. Token-level guidance, not discrete stopping. | Gamma distribution for remaining length — not a learned cost-benefit curve. | Length prediction vs. cost-quality assessment; our monitor evaluates reasoning quality, not just length. |

**Summary:** This category provides the architectural templates and theoretical grounding for our monitor-executor design. ReMA is the closest architectural match, demonstrating that separate high-level and low-level agents can be trained via RL. MGV provides the cognitive science foundation. However, none of these papers apply the monitor-executor architecture to cost-aware stopping, and none uses the monitor as a reward model for executor training. Our proposal fills this specific gap.
