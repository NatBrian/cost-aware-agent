| Paper | Our Motivation (cost-aware stopping) | Our Approach (monitor agent as reward model) | Our Solution (learned soft stopping curve) | Key Difference |
|-------|--------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|----------------|
| **GRPO** | N/A — foundational algorithm, not directly about stopping. | GRPO is the RL algorithm we would use to train both monitor and executor. | N/A — GRPO provides the optimization machinery, not the stopping concept. | GRPO is algorithmic infrastructure; our novel contribution is what we train with it. |
| **GiGPO** | Fine-grained credit assignment for multi-step trajectories — directly applicable to training a monitor on per-step cost/quality. | Hierarchical advantage estimation — could be used to train the monitor on step-level signals. | Step-level advantages — could capture the diminishing marginal utility of additional steps. | GiGPO improves credit assignment; we apply this to cost-aware stopping specifically. |
| **Training-Free GRPO** | Cost-efficient agent improvement without parameter updates — supports the feasibility of cheap monitor training. | In-context knowledge accumulation instead of gradient descent — alternative training approach. | N/A — no explicit stopping concept. | Alternative training paradigm; our monitor could potentially use training-free methods. |
| **Step-DPO** | Step-level preference optimization — essential for training a monitor that evaluates individual steps. | DPO at the step level — directly applicable to training monitor preferences over stop/continue at each step. | Preference pairs for stop vs. continue could be constructed similarly to Step-DPO's error correction pairs. | Step-DPO is for step correctness; we adapt it for step cost-benefit assessment. |
| **EntroPO** | Multi-turn preference optimization with diversity preservation — directly applicable to our multi-turn agent setting. | Entropy-regularized DPO for multi-turn trajectories — could train monitor to balance accuracy and exploration. | N/A — no explicit stopping concept. | Multi-turn optimization framework; we add cost-awareness to the optimization objective. |
| **CSO** | Critical step identification and optimization — "stop vs. continue" IS a critical step under CSO's framework. | Verification-by-policy-execution — could verify whether stopping at a given point leads to acceptable outcomes. | Critical step training with verified alternatives — could produce training data for stop/continue decisions. | CSO focuses on tool choice steps; we apply critical step optimization to stopping decisions. |
| **AgentPRM** | Architectural template for our monitor as a process reward model. | Q(s,a) for each state-action pair — directly maps to Q(stop) vs. Q(continue) evaluation. | Monte Carlo returns from each step — could train monitor to estimate the value of continuing. | General PRM for task quality; we specialize the PRM for cost-quality trade-offs. |
| **Agent-RRM** | Multi-faceted reward model producing scores + critiques — our monitor could provide both stopping signals and explanations. | Three integration strategies (text, reward, unified) — directly applicable to integrating monitor feedback into executor training. | N/A — no explicit stopping concept. | General quality RM; we add cost-awareness and stopping as the primary evaluation target. |

**Summary:** This category provides the **training infrastructure** for our proposal. GRPO/GiGPO are the RL algorithms; Step-DPO/EntroPO/CSO are the preference optimization methods; AgentPRM/Agent-RRM are the architectural templates for reward models. Our novel contribution is not in the training algorithms themselves, but in **what we train** — a cost-aware stopping monitor — and **how we use it** — as a reward model for executor training. The algorithms exist; the application to cost-aware stopping does not.
